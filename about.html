About

I am Oscar. I work in finance and administration for a UK manufacturing business, and I use this software for a living.

Updated 11 October 2026

Why I started this

Search for accounting software and the first page is written almost entirely by people with something to sell — the vendors themselves, or accountancy practices hoping you will hire them. The genuinely useful result is usually a forum thread where someone who has used both products says what they think.

My aim is to add specific, dated observations from trial sessions, with enough detail for readers to understand their limits.

What I actually do

Finance and administration for a specialist manufacturing supplier: bookkeeping, procurement, supplier management, reporting. I use Sage 50 Accounts every working day and have built reporting systems that pull live data out of it.

That matters for one specific reason. Sage 50 is widely used and rarely written about by anyone who actually uses it — most comparisons are written from a features page. When I write about it, I am describing software I have spent years in.

I also try to say what I do not know. If I have never run payroll for fifty people, or never used a product in a multi-currency business, the relevant page says so. It costs nothing to admit, and it is the difference between a credible reviewer and a confident one.

How I test

  • Every product is signed up for and genuinely used before I write about it.
  • Testing is done on sample or personal data. Nothing from my employer's systems is ever used here, shown here, or referred to here.
  • Each review states how long I used the product and on what kind of work.
  • Every review says what I disliked. A review with no downsides is an advert.
  • I do not accept payment for coverage or for a positive verdict. If a vendor ever provides an extended trial, the page will say so.

The honest limitation: one or two sessions on a trial is not the same as running a business on something for three years. Where that gap matters, the page says so rather than papering over it.

What the current reviews cover

The first four reviews cover Xero Ignite, QuickBooks Simple Start, FreeAgent and Sage Accounting Start, using a sample company. They focus on creating an invoice, importing sample bank transactions and viewing a draft VAT return. The number of sessions and dates are stated on each review. Sage Accounting is the cloud product; my daily Sage 50 experience is separate.

These sessions did not test live bank connections, real HMRC submissions, payroll, year-end work, multi-currency trading or long-term support. A feature listed on an upload screen is not evidence that its output was tested. Where a task was blocked, the review reports that gap rather than scoring an unseen workflow.

October Sage retest

On 11 October 2026, an AI-assisted retest in the same sample company completed a manual £120 receipt and reconciliation, then imported a three-row CSV (£180 received, £36 and £12 paid). Sage asked for the date order and parsed all dates and signed amounts correctly. Account categories were initially blank; Standard 20% VAT was selected on all three drafts. Categories were chosen manually, and the fictional bank fee was changed to No VAT. All three entries posted successfully and reconciled from £120 to £252 with a £0 difference. Automatic invoice matching, live bank feeds and long-term reliability remain untested. The September errors were not reproduced; their cause remains unknown.

Evidence and editorial assistance

AI tools assist with research, browser walkthroughs, organising session notes and drafting this site. AI-written notes can contain mistakes. On 11 October 2026 I corrected conflicting Xero VAT claims and recovered the earlier Xero and Sage session notes. Those notes clarify the recorded steps, but are not an independent audit of every transaction. The affected pages explain what changed.

I distinguish an initial software suggestion from a tax rate selected manually and from the calculation that follows it. A handful of sample transactions does not establish a general accuracy rate. An error encountered in one trial account does not establish a product-wide outage.
