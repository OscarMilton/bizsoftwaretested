The short answer

Pick Sage if: you value its explicit setup and compliance prompts. The September bank-task errors were not reproduced in the 11 October retest: one manually recorded receipt was reconciled successfully. A three-row CSV also imported, posted and reconciled successfully. Automatic invoice matching remains untested, and the samples differ between products.

If you're genuinely undecided: Xero completed the three sample tasks in this session. Every task I tried in Xero worked, with defaults worth double-checking. Sage's invoicing and VAT return worked; its bank reconciliation didn't function at all during testing. That's not a small caveat — reconciliation is core to daily use of either product.

How I tested them

I signed up for genuine free trials of both — Xero's Ignite plan and Sage Accounting's Start plan — using a sample company called "Test Company Ltd" with no real business data anywhere near either account. In Xero, in one sitting, I raised and approved a sales invoice, added a manual bank account and reconciled five transactions against an imported statement, and ran a full UK VAT return. I set out to do the same three things in Sage, across two sittings a day apart, but in those September sessions the bank-reconciliation task was blocked: every route into recording a bank transaction in Sage hit the same platform error, on both days, from every entry point I tried.

Important: all testing used sample or dummy data. Nothing from my employer's systems, or any real client's, went anywhere near either account. I also use Sage 50 — the desktop product — daily at work; Sage Accounting is Sage's separate cloud product, and I'm flagging that distinction clearly rather than letting the two blur together.

This covers one Xero session, two September Sage sessions and an AI-assisted Sage retest on 11 October. These are short sample-data trials. Further checks include Xero invoice corrections and another VAT period, plus Sage automatic invoice matching. The successful Sage manual retest does not establish the cause of the earlier errors.

Where they genuinely differ

Both do invoicing, bank feeds, and VAT returns — that's table stakes. Here's what actually differed once I was inside each one.

Raising an invoice

Xero needed no template setup before the first invoice. Typing an unrecognised contact name offered "Create as new contact" inline. Typing a plain-English line-item description ("Bookkeeping consultancy – September") displayed the nominal account (200 – Sales) and VAT rate (20% on Income) without a manual change. This does not establish whether they were inferred from the description or supplied by existing defaults. It blocked me from approving with no due date set, with a clear popup, and warned — but didn't block — on a missing contact address.

Sage went the other way on almost every point. It did not change the nominal account or VAT rate when the sample invoice description was entered — the category just sits on whatever default is set in your account settings (out of the box, an unhelpful "4900 – Other income"), so you're choosing every time, or living with the default. Its invoice-address requirement is stricter than Xero's — genuinely required, not just a warning — but far less helpfully enforced: clicking Save with no address did nothing at all, no popup, just a small red icon you had to notice yourself, versus Xero's specific pop-up message. On the plus side, Sage auto-calculated a sensible due date with zero manual input, where the Xero sample invoice required a due date to be selected (Xero does offer handy quick-select shortcuts once you're there, to be fair).

Sage's VAT return used the same standard 9-box HMRC layout as Xero's, and added one thing Xero didn't have: opting out of Making Tax Digital requires ticking an explicit acknowledgement ("I acknowledge that my business is not required to register for Making Tax Digital") before you can even open the return screen, where Xero just let you pick "VAT without MTD" with no such step. I can't compare the actual VAT figures fairly, though — in the September session, Sage’s return reflected the one invoice raised (£90.00 VAT due on £450 of sales), with nothing to reclaim, rather than a full month of both sales and purchases the way the Xero figure does.

What annoyed me about each

Xero: the VAT-inclusive/exclusive inconsistency between the invoice screen and the bank reconciliation screen is a genuine trap, not a nitpick — it's exactly the kind of thing that produces a wrong VAT return months later if nobody catches it. The address-compliance warning on invoicing felt slightly naggy for a quick test, though on a real client invoice that's the right instinct.

Sage: the September bank-task errors interrupted both sessions, although manual reconciliation succeeded on 11 October. The recorded invoice workflow required manual category selection and an address before saving; its small validation icon was easy to miss. Mandatory two-factor authentication also added a setup step.

Switching between them

I haven't actually migrated a chart of accounts, historical invoices, or VAT history between the two, and I'm not going to guess at how painful that is. If you're considering a switch, that's worth checking directly against each vendor's own migration documentation, or asking their support before committing — not something either of us should take on faith from a comparison page.
