Xero vs Sage: which suits a UK small business

Xero is the smoother product to test-drive, but it still gets real VAT-treatment defaults wrong two times in five. Sage leans on stricter compliance prompts — and, in this testing, its bank reconciliation genuinely didn't work.

Published 29 September 2026 · Xero trialled for one session · Sage Accounting trialled across two sessions

Affiliate links: this page does not currently carry any live affiliate links to Xero or Sage — I haven't applied to either programme yet (I do that once there's a real run of articles published, not before). If that changes, this notice will say so and link to the full disclosure. Nothing below was written to flatter either product either way.

Trialled: Xero Ignite plan, one sitting (28 Sept 2026) · Sage Accounting Start plan, two sittings (28–29 Sept 2026)

The short answer

Pick Xero if: you want the faster, cleaner onboarding and you already know your own VAT treatment well enough to catch Xero when it guesses wrong — which it did, twice, in this test.

Pick Sage if: you want a product that leans harder on compliance defaults (forced two-factor authentication, an explicit sign-off before opting out of Making Tax Digital) — but go in with your eyes open: the bank reconciliation side of Sage Accounting was broken both times I tried it, on two separate days.

If you're genuinely undecided: right now I can only vouch for Xero end to end. Every task I tried in Xero worked, with defaults worth double-checking. Sage's invoicing and VAT return worked; its bank reconciliation didn't function at all during testing. That's not a small caveat — reconciliation is core to daily use of either product.

How I tested them

I signed up for genuine free trials of both — Xero's Ignite plan and Sage Accounting's Start plan — using a sample company called "Test Company Ltd" with no real business data anywhere near either account. In Xero, in one sitting, I raised and approved a sales invoice, added a manual bank account and reconciled five transactions against an imported statement, and ran a full UK VAT return. I set out to do the same three things in Sage, across two sittings a day apart, but the third task — bank reconciliation — never worked: every route into recording a bank transaction in Sage hit the same platform error, on both days, from every entry point I tried.

Important: all testing used sample or dummy data. Nothing from my employer's systems, or any real client's, went anywhere near either account. I also use Sage 50 — the desktop product — daily at work; Sage Accounting is Sage's separate cloud product, and I'm flagging that distinction clearly rather than letting the two blur together.

This is one sitting on Xero and two on Sage — not months of daily use. Before this page is treated as final, it needs a second Xero session trying something I haven't yet (voiding an invoice, a second VAT period) and a retry of Sage's bank reconciliation once I can confirm the fault has actually been fixed, ideally on a fresh trial account to rule out it being specific to this one.

Where they genuinely differ

Both do invoicing, bank feeds, and VAT returns — that's table stakes. Here's what actually differed once I was inside each one.

Raising an invoice

Xero needed no template setup before the first invoice. Typing an unrecognised contact name offered "Create as new contact" inline. Typing a plain-English line-item description ("Bookkeeping consultancy – September") auto-suggested both the nominal account (200 – Sales) and the VAT rate (20% on Income), correctly, without me picking either. It blocked me from approving with no due date set, with a clear popup, and warned — but didn't block — on a missing contact address.

Sage went the other way on almost every point. It does not auto-suggest a nominal account or VAT rate from the description text at all — the category just sits on whatever default is set in your account settings (out of the box, an unhelpful "4900 – Other income"), so you're choosing every time, or living with the default. Its invoice-address requirement is stricter than Xero's — genuinely required, not just a warning — but far less helpfully enforced: clicking Save with no address did nothing at all, no popup, just a small red icon you had to notice yourself, versus Xero's specific pop-up message. On the plus side, Sage auto-calculated a sensible due date with zero manual input, where Xero makes you pick one every time (Xero does offer handy quick-select shortcuts once you're there, to be fair).

Bank reconciliation

Xero worked cleanly. With no live bank feed linked (deliberately — I never connected a real bank), I added a manual account and imported a five-line sample statement as a CSV. Xero auto-mapped every column from the headers, pre-matched the invoice payment automatically, and offered sensible account and VAT-rate suggestions for the other four lines — correct three times out of five, and wrong twice: it defaulted a software subscription to "Exempt Expenses" when the correct UK treatment was a reverse charge, and defaulted commercial rent to 20% VAT when rent is often VAT-exempt. Both are easy to miss if you're rubber-stamping Xero's suggestions rather than checking them.

Sage — I could not complete this test. Every route into recording a bank transaction hit the same error page ("Sorry about this, it looks like we're experiencing a technical problem"), whether through Banking > Bank account, the "Connect your bank" setup card, or Cashbook's Money In/Money Out as an alternative. I reproduced this on two separate days, from a fresh login each time, across every entry point I could find. Everything else in the Sage account — invoicing, VAT returns, settings, reports — worked normally. This reads as a genuine, isolated fault in Sage's transaction-recording module, not user error, and it's a serious enough gap that I'm reporting it plainly rather than guessing what the feature would otherwise do.

VAT return

Xero's standout moment: buying a software subscription from an overseas supplier (Adobe) triggered the UK reverse-charge mechanism automatically — a notional output charge and a matching input reclaim, correctly self-cancelling, with no manual journal needed. Most small business owners have never heard of the reverse charge; Xero quietly got it right. The one real gotcha: amounts on the sales invoice screen were treated as VAT-exclusive (net first, VAT added on top), but amounts entered during bank reconciliation were treated as VAT-inclusive (gross, with VAT extracted backwards) — same software, two different conventions depending which screen you're on, an easy way to double-count VAT if you're not paying attention.

Sage's VAT return used the same standard 9-box HMRC layout as Xero's, and added one thing Xero didn't have: opting out of Making Tax Digital requires ticking an explicit acknowledgement ("I acknowledge that my business is not required to register for Making Tax Digital") before you can even open the return screen, where Xero just let you pick "VAT without MTD" with no such step. I can't compare the actual VAT figures fairly, though — because the bank reconciliation task never worked, Sage's return only reflects the one invoice raised (£90.00 VAT due on £450 of sales), with nothing to reclaim, rather than a full month of both sales and purchases the way the Xero figure does.

What annoyed me about each

Xero: the VAT-inclusive/exclusive inconsistency between the invoice screen and the bank reconciliation screen is a genuine trap, not a nitpick — it's exactly the kind of thing that produces a wrong VAT return months later if nobody catches it. The address-compliance warning on invoicing felt slightly naggy for a quick test, though on a real client invoice that's the right instinct.

Sage: the bank reconciliation fault is the obvious one — a core, everyday task simply didn't work across two full testing sessions. Beyond that: no auto-suggestion at all from invoice descriptions means more manual choosing on every single invoice; the invoice-address requirement blocks silently rather than telling you why; and getting into the product at all required setting up mandatory two-factor authentication before any real work could start, with no way to skip it — a genuine friction point Xero didn't have during this test, even though the security instinct behind it is reasonable.

Switching between them

I haven't actually migrated a chart of accounts, historical invoices, or VAT history between the two, and I'm not going to guess at how painful that is. If you're considering a switch, that's worth checking directly against each vendor's own migration documentation, or asking their support before committing — not something either of us should take on faith from a comparison page.

Who this is not for

Xero isn't for you if you're planning to accept its suggested nominal accounts and VAT rates without checking them — in this test it was wrong on two of five reconciled transactions, in ways that would genuinely misstate a VAT return if left uncorrected. It suits someone who wants speed and will still keep an eye on the defaults.

Sage Accounting isn't for you right now if bank reconciliation matters from day one — which, realistically, is almost every UK small business. Until I can confirm this fault is fixed (on this account or a fresh one), I can't recommend Sage Accounting as a working product for that task, whatever else it does well. Worth noting separately: this is Sage's cloud product, not Sage 50, the desktop software I actually use day to day at work — the two are genuinely different products, and nothing here should be read as a verdict on Sage 50.
