How to choose accounting software

Start with the work you need to do. Use these trials to form a shortlist, then check the gaps before committing.

Published 11 October 2026

What these reviews cover: short trials using fictional bookkeeping data, with AI assistance. They are not tests of live bank feeds, payroll or long-term reliability. Read the testing method and limitations.

1. Write down your essential tasks

List what you do each month: send invoices, record purchases, import bank transactions and review balances. Add any requirements such as payroll, stock, multiple currencies or access for your accountant. Those additional workflows have not been tested on this site.

Ask your accountant which products and plans they support. A familiar workflow and available help may matter more than a feature you rarely use.

3. Run a small trial you can check

Use fictional data first. Create an invoice, import a few bank transactions and check the resulting totals. Write down the software's initial category or tax selection before changing it, so you can distinguish a useful suggestion from your own correction.

  • Does the invoice contain the information and payment terms you need?
  • Are imported dates and positive or negative amounts correct?
  • Can you explain a match, correct a category and find the entry afterwards?
  • Can you get the report or export your accountant needs?

If tax treatment matters, check it with your accountant. This is a software testing checklist, not tax advice.

4. Check the cost after the offer ends

Use the vendor's current price for the exact plan you need. Check VAT, payroll or other add-ons, user limits and any conditions on bank-linked offers. Prices in our reviews are dated snapshots.

Before starting a trial, check whether it needs payment details, renews automatically and what cancellation involves. Set your own reminder early enough to test and cancel if it is unsuitable.

5. Make the decision against your checklist

Choose the product that handles your essential tasks clearly, at a cost you can justify, with the help you need available. Treat an untested requirement as a question to resolve, rather than assuming it works because a product performed well elsewhere.

Switching from existing software also needs a separate migration check: what records can be exported, what can be imported, and what history you need to retain. Migration has not been tested here.
