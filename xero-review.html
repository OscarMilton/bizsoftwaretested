Xero review

The lightest, fastest onboarding of the four products tested on this site so far — and the one where the invoice-matching defaults genuinely need double-checking rather than trusting.

Published 30 September 2026 · Ignite plan, trialled for one sitting (28 September 2026) · Editorial corrections 11 October 2026

Affiliate links: this page does not currently carry any live affiliate links to Xero — I haven't applied to its programme yet (I do that once there's a real run of articles published, not before). If that changes, this notice will say so and link to the full disclosure.

Trialled: Xero Ignite plan, one sitting, sample data only ("Test Company Ltd")

Evidence update — 11 October 2026: The original AI-written session notes have now been recovered. They record an initial “Exempt Expenses” suggestion for the sample overseas software subscription, followed by manual selection of “Reverse Charge Expenses (20%)”. Xero then calculated the reverse-charge entries on the VAT return without a manual journal. The notes say client entertainment defaulted to “No VAT”; they describe the rent suggestion as uncertain because the landlord’s VAT position was not established. These notes support a specific account of the session, not a measured VAT error rate. No new product testing was performed.

The short version

Good for: someone who wants the fastest route to a working, invoicing, bank-feed-ready account, and who's comfortable checking Xero's suggestions rather than accepting them blindly.

Not for: someone looking for a verified assessment of VAT-suggestion accuracy; that part of this review remains under evidence review.

How I tested it

I signed up for a genuine Xero Ignite trial using a sample company, "Test Company Ltd", with no real business data anywhere near the account. In one sitting I raised and approved a sales invoice, added a manual bank account and reconciled five transactions against an imported CSV statement, and ran a full UK VAT return without connecting to HMRC or filing it.

Important: all testing used sample or dummy data. Nothing from my employer's systems went anywhere near this account.

This is one sitting, not months of daily use — treat this as a first-impressions review of setup, invoicing and reconciliation, not a verdict on what Xero is like to run a business on for a year.

What's good

Onboarding needed no template setup before the first invoice — about four questions and you're in. Typing an unrecognised contact name offers "Create as new contact" inline, and the sample invoice displayed Sales and 20% VAT without a manual change; that observation does not establish whether these were description-driven suggestions or existing defaults. Bank reconciliation accepted a CSV with a header row and named columns with zero fiddling, auto-mapping everything correctly and pre-matching an invoice payment automatically.

The recovered session notes record manual selection of the reverse-charge tax rate for the sample overseas software subscription after an initial exempt suggestion. Xero then calculated the return entries without a manual journal. Supplier and transaction details still need checking before accepting any tax suggestion.

What annoyed me

The recovered session notes record manual selection of the reverse-charge tax rate for the sample overseas software subscription after an initial exempt suggestion. Xero then calculated the return entries without a manual journal. Supplier and transaction details still need checking before accepting any tax suggestion.

There's also a genuine trap between screens: amounts on the sales invoice screen are treated as VAT-exclusive (net first, VAT added on top), but amounts entered during bank reconciliation are treated as VAT-inclusive (gross, with VAT extracted backwards) — same software, two different conventions depending which screen you're on. Easy to double-count VAT if you're not paying attention to which one you're in.

Who this isn't for

In the recorded session Xero accepted the sample CSV and matched an invoice payment. The specific VAT-suggestion accuracy claims are under evidence review and should not be treated as a measured error rate.
