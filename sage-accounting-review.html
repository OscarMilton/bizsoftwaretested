Trialled: Sage Accounting Start plan, two September sittings plus an October retest, sample data only ("Test Company Ltd")

The short version

Good for: someone who values firm compliance prompts over software polish — Sage forces mandatory two-factor authentication and an explicit written acknowledgement before opting out of Making Tax Digital, neither of which Xero or QuickBooks ask for.

Still untested: automatic invoice matching, live bank feeds and long-term reliability. The October results cover one manual receipt and a three-row CSV with manually chosen categories, not every banking workflow.

How I tested it

I signed up for a genuine Sage Accounting Start-plan trial using a sample company, "Test Company Ltd", with no real business data anywhere near the account, across two sittings a day apart. I raised and approved a sales invoice and ran a VAT return successfully. In those September sessions, the bank-reconciliation task was blocked: every route into recording a bank transaction hit the same platform error, on both days, from every entry point I tried.

Important: all testing used sample or dummy data. Nothing from my employer's systems went anywhere near this account. I also use Sage 50 — the desktop product — daily at work; Sage Accounting is Sage's separate cloud product, and I'm keeping that distinction clear rather than letting the two blur together. Nothing in this review is a verdict on Sage 50.

The original review covered two September sittings. The additional AI-assisted session on 11 October completed one manually entered receipt and reconciliation. This remains a short trial review, with no live bank connection or HMRC submission.

What's good

Sage auto-calculated a sensible invoice due date with zero manual input, where Xero makes you pick one every time. Its invoice-address requirement is a genuine hard block rather than just a warning — the right instinct for a real client invoice, even if poorly signposted in practice (see below). The VAT return uses the same standard 9-box HMRC layout as the others, and adds one thing they don't: opting out of Making Tax Digital requires ticking an explicit acknowledgement ("I acknowledge that my business is not required to register for Making Tax Digital") before you can even open the return screen.

What annoyed me in September

The bank reconciliation fault is the obvious one. Every route into recording a bank transaction — Banking > Bank account, the "Connect your bank" setup card, and Cashbook's Money In/Money Out as an alternative — hit the same error page ("Sorry about this, it looks like we're experiencing a technical problem"), reproduced on two separate days, from a fresh login each time, across every entry point I could find. Everything else in the account — invoicing, VAT returns, settings, reports — worked normally, which left an unresolved issue in this test account; I have not established whether it was account-specific, temporary, or a wider product fault, but it's a serious enough gap that I'm reporting it plainly rather than guessing what the feature would otherwise do.

Beyond that: Sage does not auto-suggest a nominal account or VAT rate from an invoice description at all — the category just sits on whatever default is set in your account settings (out of the box, an unhelpful "4900 – Other income"), so you're choosing every time or living with the default. And getting into the product at all required setting up mandatory two-factor authentication before any real work could start, with no way to skip it.

October CSV retest

The headered file contained Date, Description and Amount columns, with three fictional transactions dated 11 October. Sage asked me to select day–month–year rather than assuming the order. The preview correctly showed £180 Money in, −£36 Money out and −£12 Money out. An earlier import session timed out and had to be restarted; the fresh session succeeded.

The draft account fields were blank. I manually chose Sales – Services, Office Stationery and Bank Charges. Standard 20% VAT was initially selected on each draft; I retained it for the fictional service and stationery and chose No VAT for the fee. These were deliberately assigned test treatments, not a test of tax advice or suggestion accuracy. All three appeared under Completed. In bank reconciliation, the new cleared entries were already checked; £120 opening plus £180 received minus £48 paid produced £252 and a £0 difference, and Finish saved the reconciliation.
