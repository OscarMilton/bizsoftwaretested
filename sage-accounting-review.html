Sage Accounting review

Stricter compliance defaults than Xero or QuickBooks, and sensible invoice handling — but I still cannot tell you whether its bank reconciliation works, because it failed on every attempt across two separate testing sessions.

Published 30 September 2026 · Start plan, trialled across two sittings (28–29 September 2026)

Affiliate links: this page does not currently carry any live affiliate links to Sage — I haven't applied to its programme yet (I do that once there's a real run of articles published, not before). If that changes, this notice will say so and link to the full disclosure.

Trialled: Sage Accounting Start plan, two sittings, sample data only ("Test Company Ltd")

The short version

Good for: someone who values firm compliance prompts over software polish — Sage forces mandatory two-factor authentication and an explicit written acknowledgement before opting out of Making Tax Digital, neither of which Xero or QuickBooks ask for.

Not for: anyone who needs working bank reconciliation from day one, which is realistically almost every UK small business. I couldn't get it to work on this trial, on two separate days, from a fresh login each time.

How I tested it

I signed up for a genuine Sage Accounting Start-plan trial using a sample company, "Test Company Ltd", with no real business data anywhere near the account, across two sittings a day apart. I raised and approved a sales invoice and ran a VAT return successfully. The third task — bank reconciliation — never worked: every route into recording a bank transaction hit the same platform error, on both days, from every entry point I tried.

Important: all testing used sample or dummy data. Nothing from my employer's systems went anywhere near this account. I also use Sage 50 — the desktop product — daily at work; Sage Accounting is Sage's separate cloud product, and I'm keeping that distinction clear rather than letting the two blur together. Nothing in this review is a verdict on Sage 50.

This is two sittings, not months of daily use, and one of the three core tasks never completed. Before this page is treated as final, it needs a retry of bank reconciliation on a fresh trial account to check whether the fault has been fixed.

What's good

Sage auto-calculated a sensible invoice due date with zero manual input, where Xero makes you pick one every time. Its invoice-address requirement is a genuine hard block rather than just a warning — the right instinct for a real client invoice, even if poorly signposted in practice (see below). The VAT return uses the same standard 9-box HMRC layout as the others, and adds one thing they don't: opting out of Making Tax Digital requires ticking an explicit acknowledgement ("I acknowledge that my business is not required to register for Making Tax Digital") before you can even open the return screen.

What annoyed me

The bank reconciliation fault is the obvious one. Every route into recording a bank transaction — Banking > Bank account, the "Connect your bank" setup card, and Cashbook's Money In/Money Out as an alternative — hit the same error page ("Sorry about this, it looks like we're experiencing a technical problem"), reproduced on two separate days, from a fresh login each time, across every entry point I could find. Everything else in the account — invoicing, VAT returns, settings, reports — worked normally, which reads as a genuine, isolated fault in Sage's transaction-recording module rather than anything I did wrong, but it's a serious enough gap that I'm reporting it plainly rather than guessing what the feature would otherwise do.

Beyond that: Sage does not auto-suggest a nominal account or VAT rate from an invoice description at all — the category just sits on whatever default is set in your account settings (out of the box, an unhelpful "4900 – Other income"), so you're choosing every time or living with the default. And getting into the product at all required setting up mandatory two-factor authentication before any real work could start, with no way to skip it.

Who this isn't for

Right now, anyone who needs bank reconciliation working from day one — which, realistically, is almost every UK small business. Until I can confirm this fault is fixed, on this account or a fresh one, I can't recommend Sage Accounting as a working product for that task, whatever else it does well.
