QuickBooks review

The most capable bank reconciliation tested on this site so far — it reads photos and PDFs of statements, not just CSV, and explains its own invoice-matching decisions in plain English. Its weak spot is VAT: it stayed off by default even after I told the setup wizard I wanted to manage it.

Published 30 September 2026 · Simple Start plan, 30-day trial, trialled for one sitting (29 September 2026)

Trialled: QuickBooks Simple Start, 30-day trial, Limited Company, one sitting, sample data only ("Test Company Ltd")

The short version

Good for: anyone who wants the most help with bank reconciliation of any product tested on this site — statement uploads as PDF, JPG, PNG or HEIC as well as CSV, and suggested invoice matches that come with a written explanation rather than just a confidence tick.

Not for: someone who assumes ticking "Manage VAT" during setup means VAT is switched on. It isn't — that choice only changes which dashboard tiles you see.

How I tested it

I signed up for a genuine QuickBooks Simple Start trial (Limited Company) using a sample company, "Test Company Ltd", with no real business data anywhere near the account. In one sitting I raised a sales invoice, set up VAT and Making Tax Digital registration details without connecting to HMRC, created a manual bank account and imported a six-line CSV statement, matched and categorised those transactions, and pulled the standard UK VAT return for a quarter containing my test data.

Important: all testing used sample or dummy data. Nothing from my employer's systems went anywhere near this account.

This is one sitting, not months of daily use — treat this as a first-impressions review of setup, invoicing, bank reconciliation and a VAT return, not a verdict on running a business on QuickBooks for a year.

What's good

Bank reconciliation is where QuickBooks stood out. No live bank was connected — I created a manual account and uploaded a six-line CSV statement, and QuickBooks auto-detected the header row, the date format, and mapped all three columns correctly with no input from me. It also accepts PDF, JPG, PNG and HEIC statement uploads, not just CSV/QFX/QBO/OFX/TXT — broader than any other product tested on this site, with a note that "our team of global experts reviews some statements to ensure accuracy", which reads as OCR or AI-assisted extraction on the back end.

The standout feature: a bank line reading "Acme Test Supplies Ltd - INV 1001" for £540.00 was immediately suggested as a match against the invoice I'd raised, with a written, plain-English rationale for the match — "The bank memo explicitly states 'Acme Test Supplies Ltd - INV 1001' along with the received amount... matches the Open Balance intended for this invoice. No service charges are mentioned, so the full amount is allocated to the invoice." No other product tested on this site explains its suggested matches like this.

The VAT return was also genuinely reassuring once set up: pointed at a quarter containing my test data, every box tied out exactly to what I'd actually posted — £90.00 due on the invoice, and critically, only £7.08 reclaimed on purchases, because that's the only transaction I'd actually categorised and posted with a VAT code. The other bank lines still sitting in "Pending" correctly didn't feed into the figures at all. An unreviewed bank line can't silently skew a QuickBooks VAT return.

What annoyed me

VAT stayed off by default even after I chose "Manage VAT" as an onboarding goal — that goal only changes which dashboard tiles you see, not whether VAT is actually enabled. With VAT not yet configured, the invoice editor has no VAT column at all; my test invoice saved as a flat amount with no way to add tax until I went and set VAT up separately. Even after that, every individual invoice still defaults to "No VAT" and needs manually switching on — a genuine trap, easy to imagine someone sending out several invoices before noticing none of them charge VAT.

Once VAT is switched on company-wide, QuickBooks also refuses to post any categorised expense without an explicit VAT code chosen first — even a plain bank line with no VAT amount stated anywhere — which is stricter than Xero, which lets you post an uncategorised-VAT expense and only warns you about it afterwards. And a plain expense line ("Office supplies - Staples") got no auto-suggested nominal code at all — the AI matching seems to apply specifically to invoice- or bill-shaped transactions with a clear reference number, not everyday expense coding.

Who this isn't for

If you want VAT switched on and charging correctly the moment you finish onboarding, QuickBooks isn't that — in this test it stayed off by default even after I said I wanted to manage VAT, and needed a deliberate second trip to fix. It suits someone happy to double-check VAT status explicitly before sending a first real invoice, in exchange for genuinely the best bank reconciliation experience tested on this site so far.
